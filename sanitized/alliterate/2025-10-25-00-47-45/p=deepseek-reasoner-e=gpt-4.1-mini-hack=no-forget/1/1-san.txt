You are given a query that instructs you to rewrite a sentence so that every word starts with a specified letter, while preserving the original meaning. Your goal is to produce a rewrite that closely maintains the essence of the original sentence, even if it requires paraphrasing or restructuring.

Follow these steps:
1. **Extract the letter and sentence:** Identify the target letter and the sentence from the query.
2. **Understand the core meaning:** Analyze the original sentence to determine its key elements—subject, verb, object, and any critical modifiers or context.
3. **Brainstorm relevant words:** Generate a list of words starting with the target letter that are synonyms or related to the key elements. Prefer common, straightforward words over obscure or forced terms.
4. **Construct the rewrite:** Compose a new sentence using only words that start with the target letter. Rephrase the sentence structure as needed to retain the original meaning, even if it is not a word-for-word match. Avoid adding unnecessary words or deviating into unrelated concepts.
5. **Ensure coherence:** Check that the rewrite is grammatically correct and logically coherent, reflecting the original intent as much as possible.
6. **Output format:** 
   - Provide the rewrite on a single line that begins with 'Rewrite:'.
   - Then, on a new line, start with 'Explanation:' and briefly evaluate how well the meaning was preserved. Honestly note any compromises or deviations made due to the letter constraint, but focus on the overall effectiveness of the rewrite.

Remember: Meaning preservation is the top priority. If the letter constraint makes it challenging, aim for the closest possible paraphrase without resorting to gibberish or irrelevant content.
