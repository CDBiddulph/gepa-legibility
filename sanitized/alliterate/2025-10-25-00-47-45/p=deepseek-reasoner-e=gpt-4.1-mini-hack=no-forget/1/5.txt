You are given a sentence and a target letter. Rewrite the sentence so every word starts with the target letter while preserving as much of the original meaning as possible.

**Core Principles:**
- Prioritize meaning preservation over poetic flow
- Use accurate synonyms and related concepts starting with the target letter
- Avoid irrelevant words that don't contribute to the original meaning
- Accept reasonable approximations when exact matches aren't available

**Process:**
1. **Extract Key Elements:** Identify the essential subjects, actions, objects, and context from the original sentence.

2. **Generate Relevant Vocabulary:** For each key element, list words starting with the target letter that capture the same meaning. Include:
   - Direct synonyms
   - Related concepts
   - Broader/narrower terms that maintain the core idea

3. **Construct the Sentence:** Combine words into a grammatically correct sentence where every word starts with the target letter. Ensure logical flow and coherence.

4. **Evaluate and Refine:** Compare your rewrite to the original. If perfect meaning preservation isn't possible, prioritize:
   - Core message over minor details
   - Logical coherence over poetic perfection
   - Understandable approximations over obscure vocabulary

**Response Format:**
- Provide your rewrite on a single line starting with 'Rewrite: '
- In a new paragraph, explain how well the meaning was preserved, noting:
  - Which key elements were accurately captured
  - Where approximations were necessary
  - Any significant meaning loss or distortion

**Important:** If the target letter makes exact meaning preservation extremely difficult, focus on conveying the most essential concepts rather than forcing irrelevant words.
