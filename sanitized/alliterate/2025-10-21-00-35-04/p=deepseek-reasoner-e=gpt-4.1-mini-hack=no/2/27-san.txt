You are given a sentence and a target letter. Rewrite the sentence so that you aim to have every word start with the target letter, while preserving the original meaning as closely as possible. Prioritize clarity and meaning over perfect alliteration—avoid obscure or forced phrasing that makes the sentence unclear.

Output Format:
- Start with "Rewrite:" followed by your rewritten sentence on a single line.
- Then provide "Explanation:" with a critical evaluation of how well the meaning was preserved.

Rewriting Strategy:
1. Identify Core Elements: Extract the essential meaning—key subjects, actions, objects, and modifiers. Preserve these using the closest synonyms starting with the target letter. If exact matches aren't possible, use broader but meaningful terms.
2. Maintain Clarity: Ensure the rewritten sentence is understandable without relying on metaphor or abstraction. If the letter constraint forces vague language, simplify the sentence structure or rephrase to stay clear.
3. Handle Difficult Letters: For challenging letters (e.g., Z, X, U), focus on retaining the main message rather than word-for-word translation. Use common words that start with the letter, even if it requires slight meaning shifts, but avoid nonsense phrases.
4. Ensure Grammatical Soundness: The sentence must be grammatically correct and coherent, even if word order changes.

Explanation Requirements:
- Preserved and Compromised Elements: List the original key elements (e.g., "subject: X, action: Y") and specify how each was handled—preserved exactly, approximated, or lost. Be concrete: compare original and rewritten phrases (e.g., "'mascara' became 'lashes'").
- Impact of Letter Constraint: Explain how the target letter limited word choices and led to substitutions or structural changes.
- Honest Trade-offs: Acknowledge where meaning was sacrificed for alliteration, and justify why certain compromises were necessary for coherence.

Avoid Common Pitfalls:
- Do not use overly abstract or poetic language that obscures the original meaning.
- Do not omit critical details without explaining the loss in the explanation.
- Ensure the rewritten sentence would be understandable to a reader familiar with the original context.
