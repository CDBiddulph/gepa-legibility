You are given a sentence and a target letter. Your task is to rewrite the sentence so that every word begins with the specified letter, while preserving the original meaning as closely as possible. This is a creative constraint that requires balancing alliteration with semantic accuracy.

**Output Format:**
- Start with "Rewrite:" followed by your rewritten sentence on a single line.
- Then, provide a detailed explanation that:
  - Compares the original and rewritten versions side by side.
  - Evaluates how well the core meaning is preserved.
  - Identifies specific vocabulary limitations and how you addressed them.
  - Describes any substitutions, adaptations, or compromises made.
  - Honestly acknowledges where meaning was lost, altered, or simplified.

**Guidelines for an Effective Rewrite:**
1. **Deconstruct the Original Meaning**: Break down the sentence into key elements (e.g., subject, action, objects, modifiers). Focus on preserving the main ideas and logical relationships.
2. **Prioritize Clarity and Coherence**: Ensure the rewritten sentence is grammatically correct and understandable. Avoid obscure words or forced phrases that sacrifice clarity for alliteration.
3. **Use Direct and Simple Language**: Choose the most semantically accurate words available within the letter constraint. Prefer common words over rare ones, and avoid metaphorical language unless it directly conveys the original meaning.
4. **Adapt Strategically**: If a perfect word match isn't possible, use synonyms, related concepts, or simplified phrasing that capture the essence. Minimize abstraction and vagueness.
5. **Accept Imperfections**: It's okay if some details are lost, but the central message should remain clear. Do not ignore key concepts—find the best approximation within the constraint.

**Explanation Tips:**
- Be specific about which parts of the original were preserved and which were compromised.
- Critically assess the rewrite's strengths and weaknesses, explaining why certain choices were made.
- If meaning was significantly altered, suggest what a better rewrite would look like without the letter constraint.
- Avoid overpraising your work; focus on factual analysis.

**Common Pitfalls to Avoid:**
- Creating rewrites that are poetic or abstract but lose the original context.
- Using words that start with the letter but have unrelated meanings.
- Omitting key elements because they are challenging to fit.
- Producing sentences that are grammatically incorrect or incoherent.
